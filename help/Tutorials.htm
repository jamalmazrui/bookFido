0 - Overview

What bookFido is, what it gathers, and the ten walks that teach it.

Before you start: Nothing to set up; this walk only listens.

Step 1

Welcome. This is the first of ten short walks through bookFido. I am the host; the other voice is the screen reader, speaking as it would on your own computer.

Step 2: Insert+Up Arrow

Two reader keys help in every walk. If a line goes by too fast, Insert plus Up Arrow says it again.

Screen reader:

  • OK button

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 3: Insert+Tab

And whenever you are unsure where you are, Insert plus Tab says what has the focus.

Screen reader:

  • Audible check box, checked

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 4

bookFido gathers every book you own or have read, from up to five libraries, into one catalog you can read, search and sort.

Step 5

The libraries are Audible, Kindle, Goodreads, Bookshare, and NLS, the National Library Service’s talking book collection.

Step 6

It signs in as you in Microsoft Edge and reads the same pages you would read yourself, politely and slowly, and it sends nothing about your books anywhere.

Step 7

Then it fills in what the libraries leave out: publication years, publishers, descriptions, and author biographies, from Open Library and Wikipedia.

Step 8

The result is one combined catalog in order by title, a document for each library, a workbook, and a database you can browse in DbDo.

Step 9

Here are the walks. One, the dialog. Two, installing. Three, your first catalog. Four, reading it. Five, Audible’s companion PDFs.

Step 10

Six, Goodreads shelves and reviews. Seven, NLS and Bookshare history. Eight, the catalog in DbDo and keeping it current. Nine, the conclusion, a glossary, and help.

Step 11

Each walk is a few minutes long and builds on the last, so they are best heard in order the first time. Walk one comes next.

Something to try: List the libraries you use: Audible, Kindle, Goodreads, Bookshare, NLS. Walk three gathers them.

5 - Get Audible Companion PDFs

The companion PDFs that come with some Audible books: downloaded under friendly names and converted to accessible web pages.

Before you start: An Audible library with at least one book that has a companion PDF.

Step 1

One want: the charts, maps and recipes that come with some audiobooks, in a form I can actually read.

Step 2

Many Audible books include a companion PDF: a cookbook’s recipes, a history’s maps, a course’s slides. Audible’s own page makes them hard to find and harder to read.

Step 3

During the Audible step, bookFido downloads every companion PDF it finds, without being asked.

Screen reader:

  • Downloading 3 companion PDFs

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 4

Each is saved under a friendly name, the book’s title, rather than the code Audible gives it.

Step 5

Its web page version is made from it, so either the original or the page can be opened, whichever suits the moment.

Step 6

And each is converted to an accessible web page, with headings and text, beside the PDF itself.

Step 7: Enter

I open the companion’s web page from Downloads in the browser.

Screen reader:

  • The Joy of Cooking, heading level 1

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 8: H

H moves by heading through the recipes, the way any converted page reads.

Screen reader:

  • Breads, heading level 2

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 9

Text in the PDF reads as text; a page that was only a picture, such as a scanned map, says so, since it has no words to give.

Step 10

The original PDF is kept too, for printing or for sharing with someone who wants the pages as they were.

Step 11

A book that gains a companion later gets it on the next run, since bookFido checks every time.

Step 12

Companion PDFs belong to you as part of the purchase, so bookFido keeps them in your own folders and nowhere else.

Step 13

For a course or a cookbook, the companion is often half the value, and now it is readable.

Step 14

PDFs are converted with an engine built into bookFido, so nothing else needs to be installed for this step.

Step 15

A recipe’s ingredients, a map’s legend, a course’s outline: whatever text the PDF holds becomes headings, lists and paragraphs to move through.

Step 16

Some companions are mostly pictures; their pages hold whatever text they have, and the PDF is there for sighted help when needed.

Step 17

Because each PDF is named for its book, a list of the folder in File Explorer reads as a list of titles.

Step 18

A run with only Audible checked is a quick way to collect new companions after buying a few books.

Step 19

Companions come in many shapes: a workbook of exercises, a booklet of photographs, the full text of a speech. Each is kept, whatever its shape.

Step 20

When an audiobook refers to page twelve of the accompanying material, the web page is where to find it.

Step 21

Every companion, downloaded, named and readable. The next walk turns to Goodreads.

Something to try: Open one companion PDF’s web page, and move through it by heading.

8 - Use the Catalog in DbDo and Stay Current

bookFido Guide

bookFido is the dog that fetches your books. This is the full guide; for a quick start, see ReadMe.

Give it a moment and it will go through every book you own or have borrowed, across five services, and hand you back a catalog you can actually read: a document, a spreadsheet, and a database, all built for a screen reader.

It was written by a blind developer for blind readers, and it is part of the Homer Tools series, a sibling of urlFido.

What it does

bookFido opens Microsoft Edge, signs in as you, and visits the libraries you choose:

  • Audible — every title in your library, plus each companion PDF downloaded under a friendly name and converted to accessible HTML.
  • Kindle — every book on the same Amazon account.
  • Goodreads — every shelf of your My Books list, with your ratings, dates, and reviews.
  • Bookshare — your whole download history.
  • NLS — your reading history from BARD, the National Library Service’s talking-book and braille collection.

Then it fills in what the libraries leave out: publication years, publishers, descriptions, and author biographies from Open Library and Wikipedia.

What you get

In your Downloads folder, after every run:

  • bookFido.htm and bookFido.md – one combined catalog of every book from every library, in order by title. Each book has its year of publication in parentheses and all the details its own library offers. This is the document that opens when the run finishes.
  • A document for each library – Audible_Library, Kindle_Library, Goodreads_Library, Bookshare_Library and NLS_Library, each in HTML and in Pandoc-flavored Markdown. Each has a table of contents, an introduction with statistics and every book’s details. Appendixes list the library by author, narrator, series, genre, publisher, subject and rating, and it ends with About the Authors.
  • bookFido.xlsx – one workbook, with a sheet for each library that you can sort.
  • bookFido-companions.txt – in each folder of companion PDFs, a record of which book each PDF belongs to, so another book with the same title is never handed the wrong file.

And when DbDo is installed for you, your books go into DbDo’s BookTrail, the book list that comes with it. Each copy of each book is one row. A book you have on Audible and from BARD is two rows, each with its own format, source, narrator and catalog number, and sorting by author keeps them together. Every row carries what bookFido found: publisher, first publication, language, length, pages, ISBN, your progress, ratings, the Wikipedia article and the companion files it saved. Each author joins BookTrail’s author list with a biography. Your own entries are safe. bookFido fills only blank fields, moves a status only forward, from want to read to reading to read, and only adds tags and notes. Without DbDo, no database is written.

Why it is pleasant to use

  • It talks, without pulling you away. Timed message boxes announce each page found, each book cataloged and the minutes still to go. While bookFido or its browser is in front, each box takes focus, so your screen reader speaks it in its own voice. While you work in another program, the same words are spoken straight to your screen reader instead, and your cursor stays where it is.
  • It remembers. Everything it gathers is kept in BookTrail, when DbDo is installed, and in a snapshot of its state. So a second run never looks up what it already knows. A first full catalog can take hours; the runs after it take minutes.
  • It is polite to the web. It sends one request per second to each service and honors any request to slow down. A circuit breaker steps back from a service that has closed its door for the day.
  • It respects your conventions. Titles are in order by title, ignoring a leading A, An or The, and authors by surname. Each spreadsheet has one region at cell A1, a bold header row and a ColumnTitle01 name, so JAWS announces the column headers. A cell is truly empty wherever a value is unknown.
  • It knows a book from its twin. A title you own in more than one library is known as one work. Its details are gathered once and shared, and an author’s biography is fetched only once, however many books they wrote.
  • It keeps your business private. The log is only a debugging aid. In bookFido’s own lines, paths under your user profile are replaced before anything is written. The log’s opening lines come from the shared Homer log and name the Windows version, the user and the computer, as every Homer program’s log does.

How bookFido reaches your libraries

bookFido reads the same web pages you would read yourself. It drives Microsoft Edge, signs in as you, and gathers what each library shows its own account holder: your library, your shelves and your download history. These pages are open to you once you are signed in. Nothing is collected without signing in, and nothing is taken that you could not open by hand, a page at a time.

It reads politely. Requests to any one service are about a second apart, with longer pauses for the reference sites. When a service asks bookFido to slow down, it waits as long as it is asked. If a service declines again and again, bookFido stops asking it for the rest of the run. The aim is to be an ordinary visitor: your own reading, fetched at a human pace, without crowding anyone else off the server.

Nothing about your libraries leaves your computer. bookFido sends no book, author, list or account details anywhere. It fetches pages from the libraries you chose, and looks up publication details and author biographies from Open Library and Wikipedia by title and author name. It writes everything it learns to your own Downloads folder, and to bookFido.db in its data folder on your computer. There is no account to create, no server behind it, and no telemetry of any kind.

Installing

Download bookFido_setup.exe from the bookFido releases page and run it. It needs administrator rights, because it installs for everyone on the computer. The setup wizard asks for the folder the first time (default: C:\Program Files\bookFido); an update goes to the same folder without asking. It adds a Start-menu group and a desktop shortcut whose hotkey is Alt+Control+Shift+B.

The last page offers two checkboxes. Launch bookFido now is checked; Open the user guide is not. When you press Finish, a short results box says what was installed and where the logs are. bookFido starts after you close that box, so the box is never hidden behind it.

The program itself is in the exec folder of the installation, and this guide and the other documents are in the help folder. ReadMe.htm and License.htm are at the top.

Updating

In the opening dialog, press F11. bookFido asks GitHub whether a newer version exists. If one does, Yes is the default: press Enter and the new setup program is downloaded and started. If you already have the newest version, No is the default. If the check fails, for example with no internet connection, a message says so. The dialog’s Help (F1) ends with the same check.

Using it

Press Alt+Control+Shift+B, or run bookFido from the Start menu. An opening dialog explains what will happen and offers a checkbox for each library — Audible, Bookshare, Goodreads, Kindle, and NLS — all checked to begin with. Uncheck any you would rather skip this time; their books stay in your catalog from the last run either way.

Choose OK, and if a library asks you to sign in, do so in the Edge window that opens and then answer the prompt. Each sign-in is usually needed only once, because bookFido keeps its own browser profile.

The rest is automatic. You can cancel at any point: progress is saved every couple of minutes, and the next run continues where this one stopped.

Where bookFido keeps its files

  • Your catalogs: in your Downloads folder, as listed under What you get.
  • What it has learned: the state snapshot bookFido.json, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\bookFido\data, and, with DbDo, BookTrail, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\DbDo\data\BookTrail. A bookFido.db from an earlier version is no longer used, and can be deleted. Versions before the move to the Homer Development Kit kept them beside the program; the first run of this version moves them there, taking the newest copy when there is more than one.
  • Its browser profile, which holds your sign-ins: %LOCALAPPDATA%\bookFido\EdgeProfile.
  • Logs: one per run, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\bookFido\logs, named for the moment the run started; the 30 newest are kept. If something goes wrong, zip that folder and send it.

Uninstalling removes the program and its logs. It leaves the data folder and the browser profile, so hours of gathering and your sign-ins survive a reinstall; delete %LOCALAPPDATA%\bookFido by hand to remove them too.
